This skill guides creation of compelling product positioning, marketing messaging, and PR content for software and SaaS products. It provides frameworks, templates, and best practices for communicating product value effectively.

## Understanding the Request

Before writing, clarify these elements:
- **Product**: What does the software/SaaS do? What problem does it solve?
- **Target Audience**: Who are the ideal customers? What roles, industries, company sizes?
- **Competitive Landscape**: Who are the main competitors? What alternatives exist?
- **Unique Differentiators**: What makes this product different or better?
- **Stage**: Is this for a launch, rebrand, funding announcement, or ongoing marketing?
- **Deliverable**: Positioning statement, messaging framework, press release, landing page, or other?

## Core Positioning Frameworks

### April Dunford's "Obviously Awesome" Framework

The gold standard for B2B SaaS positioning. Work through these elements in order:

1. **Competitive Alternatives**: What would customers use if the product didn't exist? (Include "do nothing" and manual workarounds)
2. **Unique Attributes**: What features/capabilities do competitors lack?
3. **Value**: What benefit do those unique attributes enable for customers?
4. **Target Customer Characteristics**: Who cares most about that value?
5. **Market Category**: What context makes the value obvious to the target customer?

**Positioning Statement Formula**:
> For [target customer] who [has this problem/need], [Product] is a [market category] that [key benefit]. Unlike [primary alternative], [Product] [primary differentiation].

### Jobs-to-Be-Done (JTBD) Framework

Focus on the "job" customers hire the product to do:

- **Functional Job**: The practical task to accomplish
- **Emotional Job**: How customers want to feel
- **Social Job**: How customers want to be perceived

**Template**:
> When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome].

### Best, Better, Only Framework

Identify 3-5 positioning vectors where the product excels:
- **Best**: Where the product leads the market
- **Better**: Where it outperforms most competitors
- **Only**: What's uniquely offered (strongest differentiator)

## Messaging Framework Components

A complete messaging framework includes:

### 1. Value Proposition
One sentence capturing the core promise. Must pass the "So what?" test three times.

**Weak**: "AI-powered analytics platform"
**Strong**: "Cut reporting time by 80% with automated insights that surface what matters"

### 2. Positioning Statement
Internal document defining market position (use Dunford's framework above).

### 3. Key Messages (3-5)
Supporting proof points that reinforce the value proposition. Each should:
- Address a specific pain point
- Be provable with evidence (data, testimonials, case studies)
- Resonate with different stakeholders

### 4. Audience-Specific Messaging
Tailor messages for different buyers:
- **End Users**: Focus on daily workflow improvements, ease of use
- **Managers**: Emphasize team productivity, visibility, reporting
- **Executives**: Lead with ROI, strategic value, risk reduction

### 5. Competitive Differentiation
Clear statements on why to choose this product over specific alternatives.

## Writing Guidelines

### Voice and Tone
- **Clarity over cleverness**: In tech, clarity beats clever wordplay
- **Active voice**: "Reduce errors by 50%" not "Errors are reduced by 50%"
- **Specific over vague**: Use numbers, timeframes, concrete outcomes
- **Confident but not arrogant**: State value clearly without hyperbole

### Avoid These Pitfalls
- Generic claims: "Best-in-class", "World-class", "Cutting-edge"
- Feature lists without benefits: Explain what features mean for users
- Jargon without context: Define technical terms or use simpler alternatives
- Trying to appeal to everyone: Sharp positioning targets specific customers

### Structure and Format
- Lead with the most important information
- Use short paragraphs (2-3 sentences max)
- Include concrete proof points (metrics, testimonials, awards)
- End with clear next steps or calls-to-action

## Press Release Best Practices

Reserve press releases for significant milestones:
- Funding announcements
- Major product launches
- Strategic partnerships
- Executive hires
- Industry awards
- Major customer wins (with permission)

### Press Release Structure

1. **Headline**: Clear, newsworthy, includes company name
2. **Subheadline**: Expands on headline with key detail
3. **Dateline**: City, Date
4. **Lead Paragraph**: Who, what, when, where, why in 2-3 sentences
5. **Quote**: Founder/CEO perspective on significance
6. **Body**: Supporting details, context, data points
7. **Second Quote**: Customer, investor, or partner perspective
8. **Boilerplate**: Standard company description
9. **Contact**: Media contact information

### Press Release Writing Tips
- Lead with impact, not company history
- Include specific data and metrics
- Use quotes to humanize the story
- Avoid promotional language—stick to facts
- Target 400-600 words
- Include multimedia assets when possible

## Landing Page Copy

### Above the Fold
- **Headline**: Clear value proposition (what + for whom + outcome)
- **Subheadline**: Supporting detail or proof point
- **CTA**: Single, clear action (Start free trial, Book demo, etc.)
- **Visual**: Product screenshot, demo video, or hero image

### Below the Fold
- Problem statement or pain points
- Solution overview with key features
- Social proof (logos, testimonials, case studies)
- Feature details with benefits
- Pricing or CTA repeat
- FAQ or objection handling

## Output Formats

When asked to create positioning content, provide:

1. **For Positioning Statements**: Use the Dunford framework format
2. **For Messaging Frameworks**: Include all 5 components listed above
3. **For Press Releases**: Follow the 9-part structure
4. **For Landing Pages**: Provide copy organized by section
5. **For Competitive Positioning**: Create comparison matrices or differentiation statements

Always ask clarifying questions if key information (product details, target audience, competitors) is missing. Strong positioning requires understanding the specific context—never use generic templates without customization.
